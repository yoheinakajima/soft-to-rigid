Shape continuation as an alternative search for packing congruent shapes

Most searches for dense packings move rigid pieces. Hardening starts every piece as the disk inscribed in it and deforms it into the target shape while the container is pushed inward. Tested against four other searches at matched budget and tuning, it is not a better default. It is a different search: it reaches packings the others miss on squares in a square, and loses on triangles and on families held out from development.

    The paths

    A piece's state is γ·[(1−τ)·P ⊕ τρ·D]: the polygon P scaled toward its incenter, plus a disk of radius τρ, all scaled by γ. Hardening, growing and rigid starts are paths through (γ, τ) on the same schedule; simulated annealing (sa) and perturbation–compression (pc) are classical rigid baselines. Two ablations separate the parts of hardening: snap compresses disks and then switches to polygons at once, and grow-area grows rigid polygons along hardening's area schedule.
