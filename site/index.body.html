Shape continuation as a complementary search for packing congruent shapes

Most packing searches move rigid pieces. Hardening starts every piece as the disk inscribed in it and rounds it into the real shape while the container is pushed inward. Tested against four other methods at matched budget and tuning, it is not a better default: rigid starts win most often. It is a different search: on squares in a square it reaches classical packings the others miss, and it loses on triangles and on families held out from development. Every run of the study is here, replayable.

Searches, side by side

Each tile replays the best run of one method on one instance, from random start to final packing: orange pieces are still soft, blue ones are hard. Squares, triangles, hexagons and disks in squares, circles and triangles, and cubes in a cube (drawn in software, slowly rotating). Tiles are chosen by a fixed rule (largest instances where the run reached the best known value) and link to their family page. The number is the container size.

Five paths

A piece's state is γ·[(1−τ)·P ⊕ τρ·D]: the polygon P scaled toward its incenter, plus a disk of radius τρ, all scaled by γ. Every method is a path through (γ, τ).
