\section{A wider test}\label{sec:breadth}
\paragraph{Design.} Seven families: unit squares in a square, a circle and a triangle; unit triangles in a triangle and a square; unit hexagons in a square; and, as a control, unit-diameter disks in a square, where hardening changes nothing because a disk is already a disk. Best known values come from the Squares Project \cite{squaresproject}, Friedman's catalogue \cite{friedmancenter} and Packomania \cite{packomania}; truncated catalogue values (``$2.956+$'') count as reached within their printed precision. All $n$ from 2 to 30 (to 20 for hexagons) are used except five reserved for tuning, \CsevenInstances{} instances in all.

Each method was tuned twice, first by median gap on held-out instances (C01--C03; a $3\times3$ grid, extended past its edge by a fixed rule, so harden, sa and pc saw 27 settings and grow and rigid 18), then by the lowest size reached (C06; one $3\times3$ grid per method). The second round used larger instances ($n>30$) than the test set, so its settings may be less suited to small $n$; Table~\ref{tab:range} breaks the results down by $n$. Each method then ran 32 times per instance at the matched budget of Section~\ref{sec:method}, and in each instance and method the best run (if within 2\% of the best known value) and any run within $2\cdot10^{-3}$ of it, at most three, were tightened. Every campaign's plan, including its decision rule, was committed before its first run; changes after a campaign started are recorded as amendments.

\paragraph{Result.} Table~\ref{tab:main} counts, per family, the instances on which each method's lowest size is the lowest of all five. Rigid starts reach the lowest size most often overall (\CsevenRigidLowest{} instances, against \CsevenGrowLowest{} for grow and \CsevenHardenLowest{} for harden) and reach the best known value most often (\CsevenRigidSolved{} against \CsevenGrowSolved{} and \CsevenHardenSolved{}). As a check of sensitivity to the particular seeds, we resampled each instance's 32 runs with replacement (1000 times) and recounted. Within each resample, rigid's count exceeds hardening's in \BootDRHPos{} of resamples (difference \BootDRH, 95\% range) and grow's in \BootDRGPos{}, while grow against harden goes either way (\BootDGH). This measures sensitivity to the seed pool on these instances, not uncertainty about future campaigns: a resample cannot contain a run better than the ones observed, it omits a cell's single best run with probability $(31/32)^{32}\approx0.36$, which is why every method's resampled count is lower than its observed one, and tightened sizes are fixed by the original pool rather than re-selected in each resample. Over instances solved by exactly one of the two, harden against rigid is \CsevenSignRigid{} (exact sign test, $p=\CsevenSignPRigid$). The pre-registered tests were one-sided; we report two-sided $p$-values throughout, which are at most twice the one-sided ones. Simulated annealing improved most from tuning for the lowest point; perturbation--compression stays far behind at this budget, partly because its best run was within reach of tightening on only \EligPc{} of instances (against \EligHarden{} to \EligRigid{} for the gradient paths; Table~\ref{tab:diag}). To check whether that policy decides the comparison, campaign C14 tightened the three best runs of every method on \RefN{} instances fixed in advance, with no eligibility condition. Under equal tightening the counts of lowest-of-five instances are harden \RefEqHarden, grow \RefEqGrow, rigid \RefEqRigid, sa \RefEqSa{} and pc \RefEqPc, against \RefPolHarden, \RefPolGrow, \RefPolRigid, \RefPolSa{} and \RefPolPc{} under the C07 policy: the baselines gain a few instances and the order is unchanged. Without any tightening the order is different (grow \RefRawGrow, harden \RefRawHarden, rigid \RefRawRigid), so comparisons of penalty methods on legalised sizes alone would mislead. No run went below a published value, and \CsevenUnsolved{} instances were solved by no method. The median-tuned comparison (C04, Table~\ref{tab:median}) also puts rigid starts first.

\paragraph{How often does a method get there?} A count of instances hides how close each call was. Figure~\ref{fig:heatmap} shows every instance: most instances are ties; squares in a square is the only family where every instance but one that differs favours hardening, triangles lean towards rigid starts, and squares in a circle alternate. Figure~\ref{fig:bestofk} shows, for six instances, the share of $k$-run subsets of the observed 32 runs that include a run reaching the best known value. Hardening reaches Trump's $n=11$ and Wainwright's $n=19$ packings in 2 and 3 of 32 runs, and rigid starts in none; on triangle instances the picture is reversed. At $k=32$ the curve is 1 whenever any run succeeded, which says nothing about a fresh batch. Repeated batches do: on the \BatN{} instances of C13, harden and rigid each have three independent 32-run batches at the base budget, and hardening reaches Trump's $n=11$ packing in \BatSqElevenH{} of 3 batches and Schadt's $n=29$ packing in \BatSqTwentyNineH{} of 3, rigid starts in \BatSqElevenR{} and \BatSqTwentyNineR; on 29 triangles in a triangle it is the other way round (hardening \BatTriTwentyNineH{} of 3, rigid \BatTriTwentyNineR). Over all \BatN{} instances, the best known value is reached in \BatHReached{} of the $3\times\BatN$ hardening batches and \BatRReached{} of the rigid ones, and on \BatHSome{} (harden) and \BatRSome{} (rigid) instances some batches reach it and others do not. Statements that one method is ``the only one'' to reach a packing are statements about one batch of 32 runs.
