\section{Where shape continuation could matter}\label{sec:matters}
Record tables for unit squares are a clean test bed, not an end. Packing problems with the same geometry carry direct costs elsewhere, and the question this paper asks (does it help to let pieces become their real shape gradually?) transfers to them. We list the settings where we think it is worth testing, with the caveat that matters most in practice: density is rarely the true objective.
\begin{itemize}\itemsep1pt
\item \emph{Nesting and cutting stock.} Irregular 2D nesting in sheet metal, textiles, leather, glass and composites, and its typology within cutting and packing \cite{Wascher2007,Bennell2008,Leao2020}, is where overlap minimisation in a shrinking container already dominates \cite{Umetani2009,Gardeyn2025}. Hardening fits that loop without changing it: pieces start as their inscribed disks (or as the round end of any convex Minkowski family) and harden while the strip shrinks. Inscribed circles are already used there as an overlap proxy \cite{Jones2014,Gardeyn2025}, so the change is to make the proxy a schedule.
\item \emph{Container, pallet and build-volume loading.} 3D loading is box packing under stability, weight, orientation and delivery-order constraints \cite{Bortfeldt2013}; additive-manufacturing build packing couples orientation to the pack \cite{Araujo2019}. Our cube results suggest that late commitment to orientation can find tilted arrangements rigid starts miss, but loading constraints usually forbid tilt, so the gain would have to come from irregular parts.
\item \emph{Layout and floorplanning.} Two-stage ``circles first, then rectangles'' methods are established in facility layout and VLSI floorplanning \cite{AnjosVannelli2002,Luo2008}, and analytical placers already anneal a smoothed overlap penalty \cite{Lu2015}. A continuous path from disk to rectangle is a direct variant of the first; whether it beats the two-stage switch is the C11-style question asked here.
\item \emph{Materials and colloids.} Dense and confined packings of faceted particles are studied as models of condensed matter \cite{Torquato2018,TorquatoJiao2009,Wang2018}. Shape continuation is a cheap way to generate candidate dense structures in confinement, and the snap ablation (Section~\ref{sec:learned}) is the kind of control that separates preparation protocol from shape, a known source of variation in jammed packings \cite{Chaudhuri2010}.
\item \emph{Certificates.} Packing records increasingly come with exact certificates \cite{squaresproject,squish}, and packing has driven proof technology before \cite{Hales2017}. The exact enclosure-and-separation check used here is small and reusable for any polygon whose vertices lie in a quadratic field.
\end{itemize}
Resource allocation (virtual machines on servers, jobs on machines) is often called bin packing \cite{Christensen2017,Verma2015}, and lattice packings underlie error-correcting codes \cite{ConwaySloane}; we mention them only to mark the boundary, since neither has a geometric shape to harden. In every setting above, the useful result is not a lower fill but a lower cost under the operator's own side constraints, and a method that ignores those constraints produces rework rather than savings. The test that would justify hardening there is the one we ran here: equal budget, tuned baselines, and the best result reported.
